Sunflower honey is the most common and affordable wholesome honey variety. On the market it is not always valued highly because of strong competition among sellers, yet that does not make the product any less beneficial. What defines sunflower honey most of all is how quickly it crystallizes. That is not a serious flaw: its useful properties remain. The variety is not very sticky when it is genuine; over time the honey turns lighter and thickens. Below you will find a description and the benefits of this honey.

Color

Honey collected from sunflowers and extracted right away is light. As it settles, crystallization begins, the color darkens, and it takes on a mustard tone. A thin white glucose layer always forms on set honey.

There are about 320 kcal in 100 grams of honey — quite a lot. So eating a large amount of sunflower honey at once is not recommended. The daily portion should be based on body weight. On average, the comfortable daily range is 55–95 g.

Natural sunflower honey contains:

  • carbohydrates — 79%;
  • water — 17%;
  • proteins and vitamins — 3%;
  • minerals — 1%.

Minerals and vitamins

Sunflower nectar combines many vitamins and minerals — researchers have counted almost 300. The body absorbs them easily. These beneficial components of the bees’ product have a positive effect on human health. This type of honey contains the following bioactive substances:

  • vitamins C, H, K, and beta-carotene;
  • proteins and amino acids;
  • minerals.

It also contains small amounts of choline, betaine, and related organic acids. Sunflower honey includes 27 amino acids that are important for normal body function.

Benefits

What are the benefits of eating honey regularly? It can strengthen the immune system and improve general well-being. Useful effects include:

  • Support for the liver and digestive system.
  • Stronger immunity and help against colds and flu.
  • Preventive use that may reduce the risk of arthritis-related issues.
  • Support in the complex care of urinary tract conditions.
  • Possible recommendation by cardiologists for heart health prevention and care.

Sunflower honey, whose healing qualities are not always widely known, helps in the complex therapy of many conditions. The high glucose content places it among supportive foods for cardiovascular health.

Sunflower honey is also a good cleansing product that can help the body remove toxins.

Important! Regular use of sunflower honey may help lower cholesterol levels.

A positive effect has also been noted for digestion: cells regenerate faster with honey. That makes sunflower honey useful in the supportive care of gastritis, ulcers, and colitis. It can act as a mild diuretic and is used in complex kidney care.

Contraindications

Along with its benefits, floral sunflower honey also has a few downsides. For example, pollen can cause allergies in some people. That means honey collected from a sunflower field can harm people with allergies. In doubtful cases, put a drop of honey on the wrist; if an allergic reaction appears as redness or itching, you should not eat the nectar.

Honey is also not recommended during pregnancy and breastfeeding. The sweet product can cause diathesis in sensitive people. Glucose and sucrose are risky for some — mainly for people with diabetes and those actively managing obesity.

Sunflower honey for wintering bees

A wintering shed (omshanik) helps bee colonies get through harsh winters — a semi-basement space where hives are kept for the cold season.

Most beekeepers avoid leaving bees on natural sunflower honey for winter because it crystallizes quickly and can cause digestive problems for the bees. They replace it with sugar syrup, which small organisms digest more easily in winter cold.

Still, some beekeepers prefer to leave the hard-working bees a little sunflower honey for winter. A successful overwintering is likely only when frosts are not too severe and the air is humid enough. Under those conditions crystallization proceeds slowly, so its final stage comes later — and digestive problems are less likely to hit the insects.

Tip! Professionals recommend feeding bees another honey variety that crystallizes more slowly and is more nourishing in winter.

When deciding which variety to leave as winter feed for the bees, remember that sunflower honey has a very high level of vitamins, minerals, and nutrients. All of these components are simply indispensable for colony stability through the winter.

Experienced beekeepers often mix sunflower honey with sugar syrup. That reduces unwanted crystallization. Bees first use the lighter sugars. By spring, there are usually no traces of artificial feed on the comb surface — a good sign of honey quality without leftover sugar.

Choosing and storing honey

Sunflower honey can range from mustard to amber in color.

When buying honey, pay attention to:

  • Price. Honey sold much cheaper than elsewhere is a warning sign — sucrose, sand, chalk, starch, or molasses may be mixed in. Bees are sometimes fed sugar syrup and produce a mass that only looks like natural honey.
  • Color. Sunflower honey ranges from mustard to amber. An unnaturally pale tone can come from processed sugar; very dark shades may mean old remelted honey with little real benefit.
  • Taste. Genuine honey dissolves in the mouth without hard crystals and lightly tingles the throat after swallowing.
  • Structure. Dip a toothpick into the honey and lift it: real honey stretches into a thread. When the thread breaks, it settles into a small mound that slowly spreads.

In the autumn–winter period honey always crystallizes faster, while in warmth it cannot set hard. That is why its healing and nutritional properties are preserved. You need to keep one steady temperature all winter — that will protect the honey.

Sunflower honey has a pleasant taste, aroma, and color, and is easy to digest. Knowing its benefits — and how to choose and store it — you can enjoy nectar and support your health.

Buckwheat honey

Buckwheat honey is among the most sought-after varieties. It is considered a premium honey with a unique composition behind its beneficial properties.

Taste, color, and consistency

The product is made from nectar bees collect from buckwheat. In Russia, most buckwheat honey comes from regions such as:

  • Bashkiria;
  • the Cis-Urals;
  • the Volga region;
  • Altai;

Depending on where it is produced, the honey may also be classified differently.

Buckwheat honey has a number of traits that help distinguish it from other honey varieties:

  • It has a rich dark color that can range from deep yellow to dark brown. The product may also have a violet or reddish tint;
  • Buckwheat honey has a taste and aroma marked by astringency, richness, and density. It is slightly spicy and may be mildly bitter, while the aroma remains floral. Because of that astringent taste, even a small amount usually leaves a tickle in the throat;
  • This honey crystallizes quickly — it sugars. As a result its structure becomes uneven, and the crystals may be either coarse- or fine-grained. Crystallization usually happens by late October or early November. The honey is typically harvested around mid-July. Buckwheat itself blooms from mid-July to mid-August;
  • Whether buckwheat honey is liquid or already crystallized, it should not pour quickly from a spoon. Fast flow usually means the product is unripe;
  • Judging by the proper pour speed for this variety, one clear conclusion follows: it has high viscosity.
